百姓Hyakushō

How to hold a village together

Four peasants and a valley. The clock runs on its own — set the speed at the bottom, or pause with Space and take as long as you like.

  1. Days pass, and people sleep. A day is forty seconds at 1×. Villagers work from dawn, go to bed around nine, and get up at first light. Watch the valley go dark and the hearths come on.
  2. Build a room, not a house. There is no pre-fab hut. Raise timber walls in a ring, hang a door, and lay a futon inside. Anywhere fully enclosed counts as indoors: warm through winter, and the only place anyone sleeps properly.
  3. Furnish it. An irori hearth keeps a room alive through the cold and lights it after dark. A chest holds more stores. A low table is somewhere to eat that is not the floor.
  4. The valley grows more than rice. Bracken in spring; hemp and chestnuts through summer; mushrooms and wild yam in autumn. Each is only worth taking in its own season. Herbs and hemp are useless raw.
  5. Make things. Build a work bench and tap it to set what it makes: hemp into cloth, herbs into medicine, or a glut of rice dried into more than you started with. Villagers come and work it on their own.
  6. You give orders, not commands. Choose a work order or a building, then drag across the map. Villagers pick up the nearest job they are willing to do; the toggles under each name in Village decide what that is.
  7. The levy, if you want one. The daimyō's collectors come each autumn: 18 koku, then 55, then 100, then 155. Falling short costs standing in proportion. Tap the levy chip to read the ledger.
  8. Tap a villager to hear what they are thinking. Their spirit is a list of named reasons, not a dial. Fix the reasons and the number follows. Tap their name to call them something of your own.

Moving the map: drag with no tool chosen to pan, pinch or scroll to zoom, tap a tile to inspect it, tap a villager to select them, tap a bench to set its work. While a tool is chosen, dragging paints instead.

Building: blueprints cost nothing to place — draw the whole house and the village builds it as the timber comes in. Walls drag as a room outline; the Fill toggle makes them a solid block. The chip at the bottom of the map counts what you are about to place and what it will cost. Cancel calls off plans and work orders; Deconstruct marks something already standing to be pulled down for half its materials back.

Keyboard: Space pause · 1/2/3 speed · B build menu · C/M/F work orders · X cancel · V deconstruct · R fill toggle · Ctrl+Z undo · Esc put the tool down · H this page
